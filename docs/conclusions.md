# Conclusions

Summary of the peatland fire susceptibility study across five Indonesian districts for the 2023 to 2025 period.

## Answers to the Research Questions

RQ1. The strongest fire driver is ndwi. The strongest driver interaction is compound dryness (VPD with soil moisture), strength 0.087.

RQ2. Emerging hotspots: 239 intensifying cells and 78 new cells require priority monitoring.

RQ3. Mean susceptibility declined with El Nino: 0.203 in 2023, 0.097 in 2024, 0.045 in 2025. Fire counts correlate with the Oceanic Nino Index at r = 0.684.

RQ4. On high activity days, winds export smoke toward the northwest Malacca corridor. This is a wind based proxy, not a dispersion model.

## Key Findings

1. Highest risk district: Ogan Komering Ilir.
2. Model generalises across years: AUC 2023 0.62, 2024 0.74, 2025 0.66.
3. Mean spatial cross validation AUC: 0.708.
4. Compound dryness is the dominant fire trigger.
5. El Nino strongly modulates interannual fire activity.

## Limitations

1. Peat extent is an elevation proxy, not the licensed national map.
2. Model skill is moderate, a screening tool not a precise forecaster.
3. Smoke export is a wind proxy, not a dispersion model.
4. Carbon estimate and ONI values require verification before publication.
